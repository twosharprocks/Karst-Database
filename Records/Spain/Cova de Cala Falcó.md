# Source notes

(Translated from German) "In the immediate vicinity of Cala Falcó there are several cave systems that are partially accessible and have underground lakes. These include, to the west and north of the bay, the [Cova de sa Piqueta](Cova%20de%20sa%20Piqueta.md) , the Cova des Pont , the [Cova des Xots](Cova%20des%20Xots.md) and the [Cova des Pirata](Cova%20des%20Pirata.md) . On the coast to the northeast are the Cova des Moroand southeast the [Cova des Coloms](Cova%20des%20Coloms.md), accessible only by water . Directly on the north side of the bay is the entrance to the Cova de Cala Falcó , a karst cave that is no longer illuminated a few meters behind the entrance."

"Cala Falcó is a small, pristine cove beach in the municipality of Calvia in the southwest of Mallorca, about 20 minutes from Palma. Only 40-metres long, with no hotels or shops, this jewel-like beach is an ideal escape from the busier, nearby resort towns such as Magaluf and Palma Nova.

Cala Falcó boasts clear, calm waters, a small area of white sand, and low rocks on which many people place their towels as space is limited. There are some sunbeds and tiki parasols available for hire, but there are no water sports activities apart from swimming and snorkelling. This is a popular beach for locals, as well as younger tourists, especially Germans and Italians, looking for a calm day at the beach.

There are no facilities on-site at this beach and only one small beach bar but it does the trick with drinks and a small menu of food, so you won't go hungry for the day. Cala Falcó is surrounded by low-level cliffs and rocks with pine trees and bushes."

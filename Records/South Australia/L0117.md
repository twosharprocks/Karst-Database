# About

2021-March: MW checked on Foot. Pine-filled depression, no obvious lead

# Source notes

## Lower South East Cave Reference — Page 232

This sinkhole was first mentioned in CEGSA's Records in a Trip Report dated October 1968 by Fred W. Aslin and Norman Hocknell, when they reported that "it was noticed by R.W. Davies in about 1964(?)" and described it as being "... 22 feet long by 16 feet wide by 15 feet deep. The north, south and east walls are sheer Dune(?) limestone, liberally interspersed with clay-pots. The west wall is a big soil slope which is in danger of collapsing and must be negotiated with care.
"The bottom of the sinkhole is comprised of broken rock and soil which blocks what appears to be a passage dipping steeply to the south". A little formation was seen but it was stated that any additional exploration would require a major dig to prevent the collapse of the unstable sides.
